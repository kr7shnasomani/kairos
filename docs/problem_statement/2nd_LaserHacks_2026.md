# 2nd LaserHacks 2026

An online hackathon run by the Cybersecurity Program at Lasell University (Auburndale, Massachusetts), with the Soft Computing Research Society (SCRS) as technical sponsor and the WiCyS Lasell Student Chapter as organising partner. Teams pick a problem inside one of the published technology themes, submit an idea in Round 1, and the shortlisted teams present a working prototype live to a jury over Google Meet in Round 2. There is no single problem statement: the theme is open within each track.

# About

## Purpose

LaserHacks is the SCRS Chapter at Lasell University's annual hackathon, meant to bring people from different backgrounds together to solve real-world problems with technology. Its stated goals are innovation and creative problem-solving, building a community of developers, designers and entrepreneurs, connecting participants with industry mentors and employers, and solutions for social good. The tagline is *Think, Hack, Build, Impact*. This is the second edition; the first ran online on 15 and 16 November 2025.

## Themes

Participants choose a problem within any of these domains. A project that spans several should name one primary category and mention the others in the presentation.

| Theme | Focus |
|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence (AI) | Machine learning models, NLP, computer vision, predictive analytics, AI-powered automation |
| Cybersecurity | Network security, encryption, vulnerability detection, secure authentication, data privacy |
| Internet of Things (IoT) | Smart devices, sensor networks, home automation, industrial IoT, edge computing |
| Image Processing | Computer vision, medical imaging, facial recognition, image enhancement, object detection |
| Blockchain & FinTech | Decentralised applications, smart contracts, cryptocurrency, digital payments |
| HealthTech | Telemedicine, wearables, AI diagnostics, medical data analysis |
| AR/VR & Metaverse | AR and VR experiences, 3D environments, immersive education, digital twins |
| Web3 & DApps | Decentralised web apps, NFTs, DAOs, Web3 infrastructure |
| Smart Cities | Urban technology, smart transport, energy management, digital governance |
| EdTech & E-Learning | Online learning, personalised education AI, virtual classrooms, skill assessment |
| Securing the Next 250 | Future-ready cybersecurity, privacy, threat detection, resilient digital systems |

The program schedule groups the shortlisted teams into five track types: Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, EdTech & E-Learning, HealthTech, and Open Innovation.

# Rounds and format

## Round 1: Idea submission

A short document (Unstop: a 2-page PDF or PPT) covering the problem statement and chosen theme, the proposed solution and its unique features, the technology stack, and real-world impact and feasibility. It is evaluated on innovation, clarity, feasibility and relevance. The website's submission guidelines ask for a five-slide deck instead (see [Submission guidelines](#submission-guidelines)).

## Round 2: Prototype and final presentation

Shortlisted teams build a working prototype or MVP and present it live, virtually, to a jury from academia and industry.

**Presenter instructions** (Program Schedule page, Instructions tab):

- All presentations are online on Google Meet; each session has a **Join** button on its date tab.
- Join at least **5 minutes** before the scheduled time. Times are given in EDT (GMT-4) and IST (GMT+5:30).
- Keep mic and video off unless asked to unmute.
- Have slides ready and check mic and video in advance.
- Session judges call presenters one by one.
- Slides may be in **any format**.
- Each team gets **10 minutes to present and 2 minutes of Q&A**.

The About page describes the event as a 24-hour hackathon with workshops, mentorship and networking; none of these appear on the published schedule.

# Eligibility and rules

## Who can enter

Undergraduate and graduate students (master's and PhD) in any discipline, graduates from the last year, and working professionals. No restriction on age, background or specialisation.

## Team formation

- Individuals, or teams of 2 to 4 members (the Unstop listing says teams of 2 to 4 are mandatory, and its header says 1 to 2).
- Teams may be interdisciplinary and may mix institutions or organisations.
- Each team names one leader for registration and communication.

## Project requirements

- Projects must be original, innovative, and created during the hackathon period.
- Solutions must align with one or more themes.
- Teams must show technical feasibility and potential real-world impact.
- All code and materials go to the hackathon platform by the deadline.

## Participation rules

- Submit the idea by 15 October 2026.
- Be present (in person or virtually) for the event, and present in the assigned slot.
- Plagiarism, or use of pre-existing projects, leads to immediate disqualification.
- Follow the LaserHacks Code of Conduct (referenced, but not published on the site).

## Other terms (Unstop listing)

- Keep a respectful, inclusive environment; misconduct leads to disqualification.
- Open-source tools and APIs are allowed with proper attribution.
- Teams keep full IP rights; organisers may showcase projects for non-commercial promotion.
- Late submissions are not evaluated. Organisers may revise rules or timelines with prior notice.

# Submission guidelines

## The five slides

| Slide | Covers |
|---|---|
| 1. Team & Institution | Institution name and logo, team name, members and their roles, team leader's email and phone |
| 2. Problem & Innovation Opportunity | The specific problem, why traditional approaches fall short, supporting statistics or examples, the potential of emerging technology |
| 3. Solution & Technical Approach | Core systems and frameworks, how emerging technology (AI, IoT, cybersecurity) is used, what is unique in the implementation, data integration, methodology, system architecture |
| 4. Impact & Technical Feasibility | Quantified benefits, computational requirements and scalability, comparison with existing approaches, performance metrics and validation results |
| 5. Implementation & Scaling Strategy | Roadmap and milestones, deployment and maintenance challenges, scaling across domains or industries, continuous improvement |

## File and supporting material

- PowerPoint (`.ppt` or `.pptx`) with embedded fonts, at most 15 MB, named `TeamName_Institution_Category.pptx`.
- Optional: a 2 to 4 minute demo video of a working prototype, uploaded to YouTube, with the link given at registration.
- Choose one primary category; mention any others in the deck.
- Deadline 15 October 2026, through the LaserHacks platform (the registration Google Form). Late submissions are not considered.

# Judging

## Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Assessed on |
|---|---:|---|
| Innovation & Creativity | 25% | Novel application of technology; creative problem-solving; unique features; innovation in user experience design |
| Technical Excellence | 25% | Code quality and architecture; effective use of chosen technologies; performance and optimisation; security and best practices |
| Real-World Impact | 20% | Addresses a real problem or need; potential user base and reach; social or environmental benefit; economic viability |
| Feasibility & Scalability | 15% | Implementation feasibility; scalability potential; resource efficiency; market readiness |
| Presentation & Demo | 10% | Clear, engaging presentation; quality of demo and documentation; team collaboration and communication; response to Q&A |
| Completeness | 5% | Working prototype or MVP; feature completeness; user interface and experience; documentation and code quality |

## Process

Teams present to a panel of judges from industry and academia. The Judging Criteria page gives 10 minutes of presentation followed by a 5-minute Q&A; the Program Schedule gives 2 minutes (see [Source notes](#source-notes)). Judges score each project against the criteria above, and winners are announced at the closing ceremony on 22 October 2026. The site does not say how winners are chosen across tracks.

## How judges are appointed

Judges are invited or nominated from industry, academia, subject-matter experts and investors, reviewed by the organising committee for balance, checked for conflicts of interest, and given written evaluation guidelines and an orientation. They are expected to have significant experience in one of the themes, judge both technical feasibility and business viability, and give feedback.

## Judge recognition awards

Judges themselves are recognised on 22 October: Best Judge for AI Innovation, Cybersecurity Excellence, IoT Innovation, Image Processing & Computer Vision, Best Mentor Judge, and Best Judge for Identifying Scalable Solutions. Every judge receives a certificate of appreciation.
